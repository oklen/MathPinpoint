\begin{abstract}
A student checking a solution, a tutor preparing feedback, or a language model grounding its answer in retrieved text needs the page that solves \emph{this} math problem, and solves it correctly. Retrieval systems, and most benchmarks used to evaluate them, reward topical similarity instead: a page about the same concept, or about the same problem with one condition changed, counts as relevant. We introduce MathPinpoint, a dataset for problem-level, correctness-aware math retrieval. Every label follows one written rubric that gives full credit only to a page that answers the same problem with correct mathematics. The corpus holds 3.5M deduplicated mathematical web pages. The training split contains 1.85M queries extracted from those pages, either a page's own question with minimal edits or a question composed from a page that states none, and 43.5M judged candidates from three retrieval routes; each label comes from GPT-5.6-Sol or from an 8B relevance model distilled from it, and records which. The test split holds 6,173 held-out queries whose pooled candidates were judged by two LLM judges and an LLM arbiter, giving 83,833 relevant pairs. Fine-tuning Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B on the training split raises nDCG@10 on the test split from 0.441 to 0.477. \tbd{one sentence on the baseline table}. Pool bias is large at this scale: the judged pool covers 66\% of the base model's top 10 but 35\% of the fine-tuned model's, and two corrections for it place the gain between +0.11 and +0.13. The data is released under CC0, and the prompts, schemas and evaluation code under Apache-2.0.
\end{abstract}
